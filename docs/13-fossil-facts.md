# Fossil facts (47)

Source of truth: games/dino-match/src/data/roster.js. This file lists the roster and the status of fact checking.

## Verification status (honest)
- Facts were written from general knowledge and cross-checked for consistency (dates, sizes, places). They are NOT yet checked one by one against web sources: the web search tool returned only link titles in the sandbox session.
- To check each fossil, use: Natural History Museum (London) and Smithsonian NMNH fossil pages, Britannica, Paleobiology Database for ranges, and the original discovery papers where named.
- Rule for the game text: say "about", give dinosaurs vs other reptiles correctly, keep it 13+ friendly, no invented numbers.

## Notes
- Brontosaurus: named by Marsh in 1879, later treated as Apatosaurus, and a 2015 study (Tschopp, Mateus and Benson) said it is a valid separate genus. Source to cite: Tschopp et al. 2015, PeerJ.
- Pterodactylus and Pteranodon are pterosaurs (flying reptiles), not dinosaurs. The facts say so.
- Plesiosaurus, Shonisaurus (ichthyosaur), Placerias, Postosuchus, Edaphosaurus, Dimetrodon, Inostrancevia are not dinosaurs; facts say so.
- Tyrannosaurus rex is the finale (longest cutscene, bigger reveal).
- Diplodocus was dropped from the Jurassic list; Hypsilophodon is only an optional later extra.

## Roster

### cambrian

#### Trilobite (Trilobita)
about 500 million years ago (range 521 to 252 million years ago). Id: cambrian-trilobite
- A trilobite was an arthropod, a distant cousin of crabs, insects and spiders.
- Its name means "three lobes", after the three long sections of its body.
- Trilobites lived in the sea for about 270 million years and died out about 252 million years ago.
- Source status: to verify

#### Opabinia (Opabinia regalis)
about 508 million years ago (range 508 to 505 million years ago). Id: cambrian-opabinia
- Opabinia had five eyes on top of its head.
- A long, flexible nozzle with a spiny claw at the end passed food to its mouth.
- It was only a few centimetres long and is known from the Burgess Shale in Canada.
- Source status: to verify

#### Anomalocaris (Anomalocaris canadensis)
about 508 million years ago (range 508 to 505 million years ago). Id: cambrian-anomalocaris
- Anomalocaris was one of the biggest hunters of the Cambrian seas, up to about a metre long.
- Its name means "odd shrimp": its grasping front arms were first mistaken for a shrimp.
- Famous fossils come from the Burgess Shale in Canada and from Chengjiang in China.
- Source status: to verify

### devonian

#### Ichthyostega (Ichthyostega)
about 365 million years ago (range 367 to 362 million years ago). Id: devonian-ichthyostega
- Ichthyostega is one of the earliest known four-legged animals, found in Greenland.
- It had seven toes on each back foot.
- It spent much of its time in the water, and its legs were strong and stout.
- Source status: to verify

#### Tiktaalik (Tiktaalik roseae)
about 375 million years ago (range 375 to 375 million years ago). Id: devonian-tiktaalik
- Tiktaalik had gills and scales like a fish, but also a neck and strong front fins with wrist-like bones.
- It was found in 2004 on Ellesmere Island in northern Canada.
- Its name comes from an Inuktitut word for a large freshwater fish.
- Source status: to verify

#### Dunkleosteus (Dunkleosteus terrelli)
about 360 million years ago (range 382 to 358 million years ago). Id: devonian-dunkleosteus
- Dunkleosteus was an armoured fish several metres long and a top hunter of its time.
- It had no teeth: sharp bony blades in its jaws sliced through prey.
- Studies suggest it had one of the strongest bites of any fish.
- Source status: to verify

### carboniferous

#### Hylonomus (Hylonomus lyelli)
about 312 million years ago (range 313 to 312 million years ago). Id: carboniferous-hylonomus
- Hylonomus is one of the oldest known reptiles, a small lizard-like animal.
- Its fossils were found in Nova Scotia, Canada, inside the stumps of ancient trees.
- It hunted insects and millipedes in the coal-age forests.
- Source status: to verify

#### Arthropleura (Arthropleura)
about 300 million years ago (range 315 to 299 million years ago). Id: carboniferous-arthropleura
- Arthropleura is the largest land invertebrate known, up to about 2.5 metres long.
- It was a relative of today's millipedes, with many legs and a body made of segments.
- It crawled through swampy forests, the same forests that later became coal.
- Source status: to verify

#### Meganeura (Meganeura monyi)
about 300 million years ago (range 307 to 299 million years ago). Id: carboniferous-meganeura
- Meganeura was a giant insect with a wingspan of about 70 centimetres.
- It belonged to the same big group as dragonflies.
- Its fossils were found in France, and it hunted other insects in the air.
- Source status: to verify

### permian

#### Edaphosaurus (Edaphosaurus)
about 290 million years ago (range 303 to 272 million years ago). Id: permian-edaphosaurus
- Edaphosaurus was one of the first big plant-eaters on land.
- The tall sail on its back was held up by long spines with little crossbars.
- It was not a dinosaur: it belongs to the synapsids, the group that later led to mammals.
- Source status: to verify

#### Helicoprion (Helicoprion)
about 280 million years ago (range 290 to 270 million years ago). Id: permian-helicoprion
- Helicoprion had a spiral of teeth in its lower jaw, like a round saw blade.
- Its skeleton was made of soft cartilage, so mostly the tooth spirals became fossils.
- It is a cousin of today's ratfish more than of true sharks.
- Source status: to verify

#### Dimetrodon (Dimetrodon)
about 280 million years ago (range 295 to 272 million years ago). Id: permian-dimetrodon
- Dimetrodon was not a dinosaur: it lived about 40 million years before the first dinosaurs.
- It belongs to the synapsids, the group that includes mammals.
- Its name means "two kinds of teeth", and its sail was made of long spines of its backbone.
- Source status: to verify

#### Inostrancevia (Inostrancevia)
about 255 million years ago (range 255 to 252 million years ago). Id: permian-inostrancevia
- Inostrancevia was a gorgonopsid, a big hunter with long sabre-like teeth.
- It grew up to about 3.5 metres long.
- It lived just before the biggest mass extinction ever, about 252 million years ago.
- Source status: to verify

### triassic

#### Eoraptor (Eoraptor lunensis)
about 231 million years ago (range 231 to 231 million years ago). Id: triassic-eoraptor
- Eoraptor is one of the earliest known dinosaurs, about a metre long.
- Its name means "dawn thief".
- Its fossils were found in Argentina.
- Source status: to verify

#### Placerias (Placerias hesternus)
about 220 million years ago (range 220 to 210 million years ago). Id: triassic-placerias
- Placerias was a stocky plant-eater about 3.5 metres long, with a beak and two short tusks.
- It was a dicynodont, a relative of the ancestors of mammals, not a dinosaur.
- Many of its fossils were found together in Arizona, USA.
- Source status: to verify

#### Postosuchus (Postosuchus kirkpatricki)
about 220 million years ago (range 225 to 205 million years ago). Id: triassic-postosuchus
- Postosuchus was a top hunter about 4 to 5 metres long.
- It belonged to the crocodile side of the family tree, not to the dinosaurs.
- It is named after the town of Post in Texas, USA, where it was found.
- Source status: to verify

#### Coelophysis (Coelophysis bauri)
about 210 million years ago (range 216 to 203 million years ago). Id: triassic-coelophysis
- Coelophysis was a light, fast hunter about 3 metres long.
- Its name means "hollow form", because its bones were hollow like a bird's.
- Hundreds of skeletons were found together at Ghost Ranch in New Mexico, USA.
- Source status: to verify

#### Plateosaurus (Plateosaurus engelhardti)
about 210 million years ago (range 214 to 204 million years ago). Id: triassic-plateosaurus
- Plateosaurus was one of the first big plant-eating dinosaurs, up to about 10 metres long.
- It had a long neck and a strong thumb claw.
- Many skeletons were found in Germany, Switzerland and France.
- Source status: to verify

#### Shonisaurus (Shonisaurus popularis)
about 215 million years ago (range 225 to 210 million years ago). Id: triassic-shonisaurus
- Shonisaurus was an ichthyosaur, a sea reptile (not a dinosaur) up to about 15 metres long.
- Ichthyosaurs breathed air and gave birth to live young.
- It is the state fossil of Nevada, USA.
- Source status: to verify

### jurassic

#### Plesiosaurus (Plesiosaurus dolichodeirus)
about 200 million years ago (range 201 to 175 million years ago). Id: jurassic-plesiosaurus
- Plesiosaurus was a sea reptile (not a dinosaur) with four flippers and a very long neck.
- It was about 3.5 metres long.
- Mary Anning found a complete skeleton near Lyme Regis, England, in 1823.
- Source status: to verify

#### Dryosaurus (Dryosaurus altus)
about 150 million years ago (range 155 to 150 million years ago). Id: jurassic-dryosaurus
- Dryosaurus was a small, fast plant-eater, about 3 to 4 metres long, that ran on two long legs like a deer.
- Its name means "oak lizard", after the shape of its cheek teeth.
- Its fossils were found in the Morrison Formation in the western USA.
- Source status: to verify

#### Archaeopteryx (Archaeopteryx lithographica)
about 150 million years ago (range 150 to 150 million years ago). Id: jurassic-archaeopteryx
- Archaeopteryx had wings with feathers, but also teeth, claws and a long bony tail.
- The first skeleton was found in Germany in 1861, soon after Darwin's book on evolution.
- It was about the size of a raven.
- Source status: to verify

#### Pterodactylus (Pterodactylus antiquus)
about 150 million years ago (range 151 to 148 million years ago). Id: jurassic-pterodactylus
- Pterodactylus was a pterosaur, a flying reptile. Pterosaurs were not dinosaurs.
- Its name means "winged finger": its wing was held up by one very long finger.
- Its wings spanned about a metre, and its fossils are from Germany.
- Source status: to verify

#### Stegosaurus (Stegosaurus stenops)
about 150 million years ago (range 155 to 150 million years ago). Id: jurassic-stegosaurus
- Stegosaurus was up to about 9 metres long, with a row of big plates on its back.
- The spikes on its tail are called the thagomizer, a name from a 1982 cartoon.
- It is the state dinosaur of Colorado, USA.
- Source status: to verify

#### Brontosaurus (Brontosaurus excelsus)
about 150 million years ago (range 155 to 152 million years ago). Id: jurassic-brontosaurus
- Brontosaurus means "thunder lizard" and could reach about 22 metres long.
- It was named in 1879, later thought to be the same animal as Apatosaurus, and in 2015 scientists said it is a separate genus after all.
- It lived in the Morrison Formation of the western USA.
- Source status: to verify

#### Brachiosaurus (Brachiosaurus altithorax)
about 150 million years ago (range 154 to 150 million years ago). Id: jurassic-brachiosaurus
- Brachiosaurus means "arm lizard": its front legs were longer than its back legs.
- That gave it a giraffe-like stance, and it was roughly 18 to 22 metres long.
- The first skeleton was found in Colorado, USA, in 1900.
- Source status: to verify

#### Allosaurus (Allosaurus fragilis)
about 150 million years ago (range 155 to 145 million years ago). Id: jurassic-allosaurus
- Allosaurus was the top hunter of Late Jurassic North America, up to about 9 metres long.
- Its name means "different lizard", because its backbones looked different from others known at the time.
- It is the state fossil of Utah, USA.
- Source status: to verify

### cretaceous

#### Utahraptor (Utahraptor ostrommaysorum)
about 130 million years ago (range 139 to 124 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-utahraptor
- Utahraptor is the largest known raptor, about 6 metres long.
- It had a big curved claw, more than 20 centimetres long, on each foot.
- Its fossils were found in Utah, USA, and it was named in 1993.
- Source status: to verify

#### Spinosaurus (Spinosaurus aegyptiacus)
about 95 million years ago (range 99 to 93 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-spinosaurus
- Spinosaurus was one of the longest meat-eating dinosaurs, up to around 14 metres.
- A tall sail of long spines rose from its back.
- It had a paddle-like tail and probably hunted in water in North Africa.
- Source status: to verify

#### Pteranodon (Pteranodon longiceps)
about 85 million years ago (range 86 to 84 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-pteranodon
- Pteranodon was a pterosaur, a flying reptile. Pterosaurs were not dinosaurs.
- It had no teeth: its name means "wing without teeth". Its wings spanned up to about 6 metres.
- Its fossils come from Kansas, USA, where a shallow sea once lay.
- Source status: to verify

#### Velociraptor (Velociraptor mongoliensis)
about 75 million years ago (range 75 to 71 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-velociraptor
- Velociraptor was about the size of a turkey, much smaller than in the films.
- It had feathers: quill bumps were found on a forearm bone.
- A famous fossil from Mongolia shows one locked in a fight with a Protoceratops.
- Source status: to verify

#### Ankylosaurus (Ankylosaurus magniventris)
about 67 million years ago (range 68 to 66 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-ankylosaurus
- Ankylosaurus was covered in bony armour and had a heavy club at the end of its tail.
- It was about 6 to 8 metres long.
- It lived at the very end of the age of dinosaurs in western North America.
- Source status: to verify

#### Triceratops (Triceratops horridus)
about 67 million years ago (range 68 to 66 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-triceratops
- Triceratops means "three-horned face".
- Its skull could be more than 2 metres long, one of the biggest of any land animal.
- It lived at the very end of the age of dinosaurs in western North America.
- Source status: to verify

#### Tyrannosaurus rex (Tyrannosaurus rex)
about 67 million years ago (range 68 to 66 million years ago). Id: cretaceous-tyrannosaurus
- Tyrannosaurus rex means "tyrant lizard king".
- It was about 12 metres long, with a bite among the strongest of any land animal ever.
- Its teeth could be as long as a banana. The skeleton "Sue" was found in South Dakota, USA, in 1990.
- Source status: to verify

### age-of-mammals

#### Hyracotherium (Hyracotherium)
about 55 million years ago (range 56 to 47 million years ago). Id: mammals-hyracotherium
- Hyracotherium, also called "Eohippus" or dawn horse, was an early relative of horses, about the size of a fox.
- It had four toes on each front foot and three on each back foot.
- It lived in forests and browsed on soft leaves.
- Source status: to verify

#### Moeritherium (Moeritherium)
about 35 million years ago (range 37 to 30 million years ago). Id: mammals-moeritherium
- Moeritherium was an early relative of elephants, about the size of a tapir.
- It had a short trunk and small tusks.
- It lived in the swamps and rivers of North Africa; its fossils were found in Egypt.
- Source status: to verify

#### Basilosaurus (Basilosaurus isis)
about 38 million years ago (range 41 to 34 million years ago). Id: mammals-basilosaurus
- Basilosaurus was an early whale up to about 18 metres long, with a long, snake-like body.
- It still had tiny back legs.
- Its name means "king lizard", because it was first mistaken for a reptile.
- Source status: to verify

#### Paraceratherium (Paraceratherium)
about 30 million years ago (range 34 to 23 million years ago). Id: mammals-paraceratherium
- Paraceratherium was one of the biggest land mammals ever, more than 4 metres tall at the shoulder.
- It was a hornless relative of today's rhinos.
- It lived in Asia and browsed on leaves high in the trees.
- Source status: to verify

### ice-age

#### Cave bear (Ursus spelaeus)
about 50,000 years ago (range 0.3 to 0.024 million years ago). Id: iceage-cave-bear
- The cave bear was as big as a large brown bear, but ate mostly plants.
- Its bones are often found in caves across Europe, where it slept through the winter.
- It died out about 24,000 years ago.
- Source status: to verify

#### Woolly rhinoceros (Coelodonta antiquitatis)
about 30,000 years ago (range 0.35 to 0.014 million years ago). Id: iceage-woolly-rhino
- The woolly rhinoceros had a thick furry coat and two horns, the front one up to about a metre long.
- It lived on the cold grasslands of Europe and Asia.
- Cave paintings in France, such as at Chauvet cave, show it.
- Source status: to verify

#### Giant ground sloth (Megatherium americanum)
about 20,000 years ago (range 2 to 0.01 million years ago). Id: iceage-megatherium
- Megatherium was a giant ground sloth, up to about 6 metres long, as big as an elephant.
- It stood on its back legs to pull leaves down with its long claws.
- Charles Darwin collected its fossils in South America in the 1830s.
- Source status: to verify

#### Irish elk (Megaloceros giganteus)
about 20,000 years ago (range 0.4 to 0.0077 million years ago). Id: iceage-irish-elk
- The Irish elk was not an elk but a giant deer, about 2 metres tall at the shoulder.
- Its antlers could span almost 4 metres.
- Its skeletons are often found in the bogs of Ireland.
- Source status: to verify

#### Sabre-toothed cat (Smilodon fatalis)
about 20,000 years ago (range 2.5 to 0.01 million years ago). Id: iceage-smilodon
- Smilodon was a big cat with huge sabre-like teeth about 18 centimetres long.
- It was not a tiger: it had a short tail and a very strong, muscular body.
- Thousands were found in the La Brea Tar Pits in Los Angeles. It is the state fossil of California.
- Source status: to verify

#### Woolly mammoth (Mammuthus primigenius)
about 20,000 years ago (range 0.7 to 0.004 million years ago). Id: iceage-woolly-mammoth
- The woolly mammoth had shaggy hair and tusks up to about 4 metres long.
- Frozen mammoths have been found in the permafrost of Siberia.
- The last small group lived on Wrangel Island until about 4,000 years ago, when the pyramids of Egypt already stood.
- Source status: to verify

### early-humans

#### Australopithecus afarensis (Australopithecus afarensis)
about 3.2 million years ago (range 3.9 to 3 million years ago). Id: humans-australopithecus
- "Lucy", found in Ethiopia in 1974, is the most famous Australopithecus afarensis skeleton.
- She was only about 1.1 metres tall, yet she walked upright on two legs.
- Her skull was small, about the size of a chimpanzee's brain case.
- Source status: to verify

#### Homo erectus (Homo erectus)
about 1.5 million years ago (range 1.9 to 0.11 million years ago). Id: humans-homo-erectus
- Homo erectus means "upright man".
- It was one of the first human species to spread out of Africa, as far as Asia.
- It made stone hand axes and probably used fire.
- Source status: to verify

#### Neanderthal (Homo neanderthalensis)
about 100,000 years ago (range 0.43 to 0.04 million years ago). Id: humans-neanderthal
- Neanderthals were close cousins of modern humans, not our ancestors.
- They made tools and used fire. The first skeleton was found in the Neander Valley in Germany in 1856.
- Many people alive today carry a small amount of Neanderthal DNA.
- Source status: to verify
