# Fossil facts (47)

Source of truth: games/dino-match/src/data/roster.js. This file lists every fossil, its facts and the sources they were checked against.

## How it was checked
Each fact was compared with the page(s) listed under the fossil (fetched with WebFetch in the verification pass). Where a source disagreed, the fact in roster.js was changed. Dates and sizes are shown as "about" because sources give ranges.

## Corrections made in the verification pass
- Trilobite: "distant cousin of crabs, insects and spiders" changed to "same big animal group" (the exact relation is debated); "about 270 million years" changed to "more than 250 million years" (AMNH).
- Opabinia: food-passing nozzle is "probably" (source hedges); length 4 to 7 cm.
- Anomalocaris: name is "abnormal shrimp" (not "odd shrimp"); the front arms were first thought to be shrimp tails.
- Ichthyostega: "one of the first" four-limbed animals; front legs were strong, hind legs smaller (was "strong and stout legs").
- Tiktaalik: also named in 2006; age shown as 375 to 385 mya (sources differ).
- Dunkleosteus: "no true teeth", about 4 m long.
- Hylonomus: "one of the earliest known" on the reptile side (a 2025 analysis questions "oldest reptile"); diet is "probably"; age 318 mya.
- Arthropleura: forests and floodplains (newer research says not only swamps).
- Helicoprion: "spiral saw", not a circular saw; only a very distant cousin of ratfish, not a true shark.
- Inostrancevia: the 252 mya extinction now says "gorgonopsids died out", not "just before".
- Eoraptor: 1.2 m long; name is "dawn plunderer".
- Placerias: removed the unsupported 3.5 m length; the two tusk-like growths are not true tusks; it is a synapsid.
- Postosuchus: 5 to 6 m long (was 4 to 5); name means "crocodile from Post".
- Coelophysis: removed the unsupported "hollow like a bird's" link.
- Plateosaurus: "strong thumb claw" changed to "large claws on its hands".
- Plesiosaurus: "long, slender neck" (was "very long"); age about 195 mya (Early Jurassic).
- Dryosaurus: name means "tree lizard", not "oak lizard"; up to about 3 m.
- Stegosaurus: Colorado state FOSSIL (not "dinosaur"). Thagomizer: coined by Gary Larson in The Far Side, 1982 (Wikipedia: Thagomizer).
- Allosaurus: up to about 9.7 m.
- Utahraptor: "one of the largest" raptors, 6 to 7 m; age 139 to 134 mya.
- Pteranodon: wingspan about 6 m, some over 7 m.
- Velociraptor: "probably had feathers" (inferred, with quill bumps on one forearm bone).
- Tyrannosaurus: strongest bite of any known land animal; biggest tooth about 30 cm with the root (the old "banana" line was dropped). Sue: found near Faith, South Dakota, 1990 (Field Museum).
- Hyracotherium replaced by Eohippus, the "dawn horse" (id mammals-eohippus): the type species of Hyracotherium is now seen as a palaeothere, not a horse. Eohippus is the genus placed in the horse family.
- Moeritherium: it had no real trunk, only a small flexible nose and lip.
- Basilosaurus: up to about 20 m, eel-like body.
- Paraceratherium: about 4.8 m at the shoulder; range China to Europe; "high leaves" dropped.
- Cave bear: "as big as the largest bears alive today".
- Smilodon: canines up to about 28 cm in the biggest species (was 18 cm).
- Irish elk: antlers span more than 4 m.
- Woolly mammoth: frozen finds also in Alaska.
- Lucy: small brain like a chimpanzee's.
- Homo erectus: "may have used fire" (fire use is debated).
- Neanderthal: "close cousins" only (they interbred with modern humans); about 2% DNA in people with non-African ancestry; first fossil recognised as Neanderthal, 1856.

## Still weaker (flagged)
- Ichthyosaurs "breathed air": general knowledge, the fetched page does not state it. Live birth is on the Ichthyosaurus page.
- Chauvet cave rhinos: not stated on the fetched Wikipedia page; widely known, to be confirmed with a cave-art source.
- Moeritherium, Basilosaurus and other age ranges in "mya": the geological epoch is sourced; the exact numbers follow Wikipedia infoboxes and may differ by a few million years between sources.
- Hylonomus and Velociraptor feather facts are hedged in the sources; the game text uses "probably".
- Not fetched or unconfirmed: the Wikipedia pages for Tiktaalik, Jurassic ages of Brachiosaurus (holotype 154 to 153 mya) and dates in the game's "when" text.

## Notes
- Brontosaurus: named by Marsh in 1879, later treated as Apatosaurus, and a 2015 study (Tschopp, Mateus and Benson) said it is a valid separate genus (Wikipedia: Brontosaurus).
- Pterodactylus and Pteranodon are pterosaurs, not dinosaurs. The facts say so.
- Plesiosaurus, Shonisaurus, Placerias, Postosuchus, Edaphosaurus, Dimetrodon and Inostrancevia are not dinosaurs; facts say so.
- Tyrannosaurus rex is the finale. Diplodocus was dropped; Hypsilophodon is only an optional later extra.

## Roster

### cambrian

#### Trilobite (Trilobita)
about 500 million years ago; range 521 to 252 million years ago. Id: cambrian-trilobite
- A trilobite was an arthropod, in the same big animal group as crabs, insects and spiders.
- Its name means "three lobes", after the three long sections of its body.
- Trilobites lived in the sea for more than 250 million years and died out about 252 million years ago.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobite; https://www.amnh.org/explore/videos/dinosaurs-and-fossils/trilobites-mass-extinctions

#### Opabinia (Opabinia regalis)
about 508 million years ago; range 508 to 505 million years ago. Id: cambrian-opabinia
- Opabinia had five eyes on top of its head.
- A long, flexible nozzle with a spiny claw at the end probably passed food to its mouth.
- It was only about 4 to 7 centimetres long and is known from the Burgess Shale in Canada.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opabinia

#### Anomalocaris (Anomalocaris canadensis)
about 508 million years ago; range 508 to 505 million years ago. Id: cambrian-anomalocaris
- Anomalocaris was one of the biggest hunters of the Cambrian seas, up to about a metre long.
- Its name means "abnormal shrimp": its grasping front arms were first mistaken for the tails of shrimp-like animals.
- Famous fossils come from the Burgess Shale in Canada and from Chengjiang in China.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomalocaris

### devonian

#### Ichthyostega (Ichthyostega)
about 365 million years ago; range 365 to 360 million years ago. Id: devonian-ichthyostega
- Ichthyostega is one of the first four-limbed animals in the fossil record, found in Greenland.
- It had seven toes on each back foot.
- It lived in shallow water and could move on land. Its front legs were strong, but its back legs were smaller.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyostega

#### Tiktaalik (Tiktaalik roseae)
about 380 million years ago; range 385 to 375 million years ago. Id: devonian-tiktaalik
- Tiktaalik had gills and scales like a fish, but also a neck and strong front fins with wrist-like bones.
- It was found in 2004 on Ellesmere Island in northern Canada, and named in 2006.
- Its name comes from an Inuktitut word for a large freshwater fish.
- Sources: https://thecanadianencyclopedia.ca/en/article/tiktaalik; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiktaalik (not fetched; age differs between sources, 375 to 385 mya)

#### Dunkleosteus (Dunkleosteus terrelli)
about 360 million years ago; range 382 to 358 million years ago. Id: devonian-dunkleosteus
- Dunkleosteus was an armoured fish about 4 metres long and a top hunter of its time.
- It had no true teeth: sharp bony blades in its jaws sliced through prey.
- Studies suggest it had one of the strongest bites of any fish.
- Sources: https://www.discoverwildlife.com/prehistoric-life/dunkleosteus-prehistoric

### carboniferous

#### Hylonomus (Hylonomus lyelli)
about 318 million years ago; range 318 to 312 million years ago. Id: carboniferous-hylonomus
- Hylonomus is one of the earliest known animals on the reptile side of the family tree, a small lizard-like animal about 20 to 25 centimetres long.
- Its fossils were found in Nova Scotia, Canada, inside the stumps of ancient trees.
- It probably hunted small animals such as insects and millipedes in the coal-age forests.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hylonomus

#### Arthropleura (Arthropleura)
about 300 million years ago; range 346 to 290 million years ago. Id: carboniferous-arthropleura
- Arthropleura is the largest land invertebrate known, up to about 2.5 metres long.
- It was a relative of today's millipedes, with many legs and a body made of segments.
- It lived in the forests and floodplains of the coal age, in Europe and North America.
- Sources: https://discoverwildlife.com/prehistoric-life/arthropleura

#### Meganeura (Meganeura monyi)
about 300 million years ago; range 307 to 299 million years ago. Id: carboniferous-meganeura
- Meganeura was a giant insect with a wingspan of about 70 centimetres.
- It belonged to the same big group as dragonflies.
- Its fossils were found in France, and it hunted other insects in the air.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meganeura

### permian

#### Edaphosaurus (Edaphosaurus)
about 290 million years ago; range 303 to 272 million years ago. Id: permian-edaphosaurus
- Edaphosaurus was one of the first big plant-eaters on land.
- The tall sail on its back was held up by long spines with little crossbars.
- It was not a dinosaur: it belongs to the synapsids, the group that later led to mammals.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edaphosaurus

#### Helicoprion (Helicoprion)
about 280 million years ago; range 290 to 270 million years ago. Id: permian-helicoprion
- Helicoprion had a spiral of teeth in its lower jaw, like a spiral saw blade.
- Its skeleton was made of soft cartilage, so mostly the tooth spirals became fossils.
- It was not a true shark: it belongs to the group of ratfish, but only as a very distant cousin.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicoprion

#### Dimetrodon (Dimetrodon)
about 280 million years ago; range 294 to 271 million years ago. Id: permian-dimetrodon
- Dimetrodon was not a dinosaur: it lived about 40 million years before the first dinosaurs.
- It belongs to the synapsids, the group that includes mammals.
- Its name means "two measures of teeth", and its sail was made of long spines of its backbone.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimetrodon

#### Inostrancevia (Inostrancevia)
about 255 million years ago; range 255 to 252 million years ago. Id: permian-inostrancevia
- Inostrancevia was a gorgonopsid, a big hunter with long sabre-like teeth.
- It grew up to about 3.5 metres long.
- Gorgonopsids died out in the biggest mass extinction ever, about 252 million years ago.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inostrancevia

### triassic

#### Eoraptor (Eoraptor lunensis)
about 231 million years ago; range 231 to 228 million years ago. Id: triassic-eoraptor
- Eoraptor is one of the earliest known dinosaurs, about 1.2 metres long.
- Its name means "dawn plunderer".
- Its fossils were found in Argentina.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eoraptor

#### Placerias (Placerias hesternus)
about 220 million years ago; range 230 to 215 million years ago. Id: triassic-placerias
- Placerias was a stocky plant-eater with a beak and two tusk-like growths on its upper jaw (they were not true teeth).
- It was a dicynodont, a synapsid (the group that includes mammals), not a dinosaur.
- More than 1,600 of its bones were found together in one quarry in Arizona, USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placerias

#### Postosuchus (Postosuchus kirkpatricki)
about 220 million years ago; range 228 to 210 million years ago. Id: triassic-postosuchus
- Postosuchus was one of the top hunters of its time, about 5 to 6 metres long.
- It belonged to the crocodile side of the family tree, not to the dinosaurs.
- Its name means "crocodile from Post", after the Post Quarry in Texas, USA, where it was found.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postosuchus

#### Coelophysis (Coelophysis bauri)
about 210 million years ago; range 215 to 201 million years ago. Id: triassic-coelophysis
- Coelophysis was a light, fast hunter up to about 3 metres long.
- Its name means "hollow form".
- Hundreds of skeletons were found together at Ghost Ranch in New Mexico, USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coelophysis

#### Plateosaurus (Plateosaurus engelhardti)
about 210 million years ago; range 214 to 204 million years ago. Id: triassic-plateosaurus
- Plateosaurus was one of the first big plant-eating dinosaurs, up to about 10 metres long.
- It had a long, flexible neck and large claws on its hands.
- Many skeletons were found in Germany, Switzerland and France.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateosaurus

#### Shonisaurus (Shonisaurus popularis)
about 215 million years ago; range 225 to 210 million years ago. Id: triassic-shonisaurus
- Shonisaurus was an ichthyosaur, a sea reptile (not a dinosaur) up to about 15 metres long.
- Ichthyosaurs breathed air and gave birth to live young.
- It is the state fossil of Nevada, USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shonisaurus; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichthyosaurus (live birth)

### jurassic

#### Plesiosaurus (Plesiosaurus dolichodeirus)
about 195 million years ago; range 199 to 190 million years ago. Id: jurassic-plesiosaurus
- Plesiosaurus was a sea reptile (not a dinosaur) with four flippers and a long, slender neck.
- It was about 3.5 metres long.
- Mary Anning found a complete skeleton near Lyme Regis, England, in 1823.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plesiosaurus

#### Dryosaurus (Dryosaurus altus)
about 150 million years ago; range 156 to 147 million years ago. Id: jurassic-dryosaurus
- Dryosaurus was a small, fast plant-eater, up to about 3 metres long, that ran on two long legs.
- Its name means "tree lizard".
- Its fossils were found in the Morrison Formation in the western USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dryosaurus

#### Archaeopteryx (Archaeopteryx lithographica)
about 150 million years ago; range 151 to 148 million years ago. Id: jurassic-archaeopteryx
- Archaeopteryx had wings with feathers, but also teeth, claws and a long bony tail.
- The first skeleton was found in Germany in 1861, soon after Darwin's book on evolution.
- It was roughly the size of a raven.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeopteryx

#### Pterodactylus (Pterodactylus antiquus)
about 150 million years ago; range 151 to 148 million years ago. Id: jurassic-pterodactylus
- Pterodactylus was a pterosaur, a flying reptile. Pterosaurs were not dinosaurs.
- Its name means "winged finger": its wing was held up by one very long finger.
- Its wings spanned about a metre, and its fossils are from Germany.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pterodactylus

#### Stegosaurus (Stegosaurus stenops)
about 150 million years ago; range 155 to 150 million years ago. Id: jurassic-stegosaurus
- Stegosaurus was up to about 9 metres long, with a row of big plates on its back.
- The spikes on its tail are called the thagomizer, a name from a 1982 Far Side cartoon.
- It is the state fossil of Colorado, USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stegosaurus; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thagomizer

#### Brontosaurus (Brontosaurus excelsus)
about 150 million years ago; range 156 to 146 million years ago. Id: jurassic-brontosaurus
- Brontosaurus means "thunder lizard" and could reach about 22 metres long.
- It was named in 1879, later thought to be the same animal as Apatosaurus, and in 2015 scientists said it is a separate genus after all.
- It lived in the Morrison Formation of the western USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brontosaurus

#### Brachiosaurus (Brachiosaurus altithorax)
about 150 million years ago; range 154 to 150 million years ago. Id: jurassic-brachiosaurus
- Brachiosaurus means "arm lizard": its front legs were longer than its back legs.
- That gave it a giraffe-like stance, and it was roughly 18 to 22 metres long.
- The first skeleton was found in Colorado, USA, in 1900.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachiosaurus

#### Allosaurus (Allosaurus fragilis)
about 150 million years ago; range 155 to 145 million years ago. Id: jurassic-allosaurus
- Allosaurus was a top hunter of the Late Jurassic, up to about 9.7 metres long.
- Its name means "different lizard", because its backbones looked different from others known at the time.
- It is the state fossil of Utah, USA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allosaurus

### cretaceous

#### Utahraptor (Utahraptor ostrommaysorum)
about 135 million years ago; range 139 to 134 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-utahraptor
- Utahraptor is one of the largest known raptors, about 6 to 7 metres long.
- It had a big curved claw, more than 20 centimetres long, on each foot.
- Its fossils were found in Utah, USA, and it was named in 1993.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utahraptor

#### Spinosaurus (Spinosaurus aegyptiacus)
about 95 million years ago; range 100 to 94 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-spinosaurus
- Spinosaurus was one of the biggest meat-eating dinosaurs, up to around 14 metres long.
- A tall sail of long spines rose from its back.
- It had a paddle-like tail and may have hunted in water. Its fossils come from Egypt and Morocco.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurus

#### Pteranodon (Pteranodon longiceps)
about 85 million years ago; range 86 to 84 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-pteranodon
- Pteranodon was a pterosaur, a flying reptile. Pterosaurs were not dinosaurs.
- It had no teeth: its name means "wing without teeth". Its wings spanned about 6 metres, and some reached over 7 metres.
- Its fossils come from Kansas, USA, where a shallow sea once lay.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pteranodon

#### Velociraptor (Velociraptor mongoliensis)
about 75 million years ago; range 75 to 71 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-velociraptor
- Velociraptor was about the size of a turkey, much smaller than in the films.
- It probably had feathers: a forearm bone shows quill bumps, where feathers attach.
- A famous fossil from Mongolia shows one locked in a fight with a Protoceratops.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velociraptor

#### Ankylosaurus (Ankylosaurus magniventris)
about 67 million years ago; range 68 to 66 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-ankylosaurus
- Ankylosaurus was covered in bony armour and had a heavy club at the end of its tail.
- It was about 6 to 8 metres long.
- It lived at the very end of the age of dinosaurs in western North America.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankylosaurus

#### Triceratops (Triceratops horridus)
about 67 million years ago; range 69 to 66 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-triceratops
- Triceratops means "three-horned face".
- Its skull could be about 2 metres long, among the largest skulls of any land animal.
- It lived at the very end of the age of dinosaurs in western North America.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triceratops

#### Tyrannosaurus rex (Tyrannosaurus rex)
about 67 million years ago; range 69 to 66 million years ago. Id: cretaceous-tyrannosaurus
- Tyrannosaurus rex means "tyrant lizard king".
- It was about 12 metres long, with the strongest bite of any known land animal.
- Its biggest tooth found was about 30 centimetres long with the root. The skeleton "Sue" was found in South Dakota, USA, in 1990.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrannosaurus; https://www.fieldmuseum.org/blog/sue-t-rex

### age-of-mammals

#### Dawn horse (Eohippus angustidens)
about 55 million years ago; range 56 to 48 million years ago. Id: mammals-eohippus
- Eohippus, the "dawn horse", was one of the earliest members of the horse family.
- It was a small, fox-sized animal, about 30 centimetres tall at the shoulder.
- It had four toes on each front foot and three on each back foot.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eohippus; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyracotherium

#### Moeritherium (Moeritherium)
about 35 million years ago; range 37 to 30 million years ago. Id: mammals-moeritherium
- Moeritherium was an early relative of elephants, about 70 centimetres tall at the shoulder.
- It had no real trunk, only a small flexible nose and upper lip, and short tusks.
- It lived in the swamps and rivers of North Africa; its fossils were found in Egypt.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeritherium

#### Basilosaurus (Basilosaurus isis)
about 38 million years ago; range 41 to 34 million years ago. Id: mammals-basilosaurus
- Basilosaurus was an early whale up to about 20 metres long, with a long, eel-like body.
- It still had tiny back legs.
- Its name means "king lizard", because it was first mistaken for a reptile.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilosaurus

#### Paraceratherium (Paraceratherium)
about 30 million years ago; range 34 to 23 million years ago. Id: mammals-paraceratherium
- Paraceratherium was one of the biggest land mammals ever, about 4.8 metres tall at the shoulder.
- It was a hornless relative of today's rhinos.
- Its fossils are found from China to Europe, and it browsed on leaves and soft plants.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraceratherium

### ice-age

#### Cave bear (Ursus spelaeus)
about 50,000 years ago; range 0.13 to 0.024 million years ago. Id: iceage-cave-bear
- The cave bear was as big as the largest bears alive today, but it ate mostly plants.
- Its bones are often found in caves across Europe, where it slept through the winter.
- It died out about 24,000 years ago.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_bear

#### Woolly rhinoceros (Coelodonta antiquitatis)
about 30,000 years ago; range 0.45 to 0.014 million years ago. Id: iceage-woolly-rhino
- The woolly rhinoceros had a thick furry coat and two horns, the front one about a metre long or more.
- It lived on the cold grasslands of northern Europe and Asia.
- Cave paintings in France, such as at Chauvet cave, show it.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolly_rhinoceros

#### Giant ground sloth (Megatherium americanum)
about 20,000 years ago; range 2 to 0.012 million years ago. Id: iceage-megatherium
- Megatherium was a giant ground sloth, about 6 metres long and as heavy as an elephant.
- It could rear up on its back legs to pull down leaves with its long claws.
- Charles Darwin collected its fossils in South America in the 1830s.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megatherium

#### Irish elk (Megaloceros giganteus)
about 20,000 years ago; range 0.4 to 0.0077 million years ago. Id: iceage-irish-elk
- The Irish elk was not an elk but a giant deer, about 2 metres tall at the shoulder.
- Its antlers could span more than 4 metres.
- Its skeletons are often found in the bogs of Ireland.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_elk

#### Sabre-toothed cat (Smilodon fatalis)
about 20,000 years ago; range 2.5 to 0.01 million years ago. Id: iceage-smilodon
- Smilodon was a big cat with huge sabre-like teeth, up to about 28 centimetres long in the biggest species.
- It was not a tiger: it had a short tail and a very strong, muscular body.
- Many thousands of bones were found in the La Brea Tar Pits in Los Angeles. It is the state fossil of California.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smilodon

#### Woolly mammoth (Mammuthus primigenius)
about 20,000 years ago; range 0.4 to 0.004 million years ago. Id: iceage-woolly-mammoth
- The woolly mammoth had shaggy hair and tusks up to about 4 metres long.
- Frozen mammoths have been found in the permafrost of Siberia and Alaska.
- The last small group lived on Wrangel Island until about 4,000 years ago, when the pyramids of Egypt already stood.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolly_mammoth

### early-humans

#### Australopithecus afarensis (Australopithecus afarensis)
about 3.2 million years ago; range 3.9 to 3 million years ago. Id: humans-australopithecus
- "Lucy", found in Ethiopia in 1974, is the most famous Australopithecus afarensis skeleton.
- She was only about 1.1 metres tall, yet she walked upright on two legs.
- She had a small brain, like a chimpanzee's.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucy_(hominid)

#### Homo erectus (Homo erectus)
about 1.5 million years ago; range 2 to 0.1 million years ago. Id: humans-homo-erectus
- Homo erectus means "upright man".
- It was one of the first human species to spread out of Africa, as far as Asia.
- It made stone tools such as hand axes and may have used fire.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_erectus

#### Neanderthal (Homo neanderthalensis)
about 100,000 years ago; range 0.24 to 0.04 million years ago. Id: humans-neanderthal
- Neanderthals were close cousins of modern humans.
- They made tools and used fire. The first fossil recognised as a Neanderthal was found in the Neander Valley in Germany in 1856.
- People with ancestry from outside Africa carry about 2% Neanderthal DNA.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal
